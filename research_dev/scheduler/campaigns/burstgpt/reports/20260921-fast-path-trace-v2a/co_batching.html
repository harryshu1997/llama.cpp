One forward pass, one phone call, several tokens

A decode slot whose feed-forward layers run on the phone cannot share a batch with a slot that keeps them on the host, so the two alternate. That single constraint decided every energy result for three days. Here is what it does, and what happened when it was removed.

Where this leaves the target. The long-decode pair is the only current result that is both strictly token-exact and a real saving: 19.2 % of host energy, all 3,267 output tokens identical, every latency bound met. No current arm passes 25 %. The historical pair that does, at 25.8 %, ran on an older deployment and is not a current measurement.

What co-batching has proven so far. The rows do share a call: 442 multi-row calls with cohorts of two and three, matched against forward and USB records, and the windows at two or more slots measure 38 J per token instead of 128 to 138. What it has not produced is a complete arm. Coverage stalled at 4 of 15 Qwen requests because 120 coherence decisions selected a zero fraction, and the run aborted before its energy was accounted.
